## Overview

Veritas Fidei is a site for Catholics who want to understand what the Church teaches, why, and where it is found in Scripture and Tradition. It has more than 50 published articles, organized into 11 topics in the order of the Catechism and 11 study tracks (articles in sequence on one subject), plus **Defending the faith**, with answers to objections.

## Features

- Study tracks with their steps in order, total reading time and "Start the track".
- Sacred art on every article, topic and track, with the work's title, artist, date and collection in the caption, and an image credits page.
- Apologetics articles in the medieval disputed-question format: "It seems that…", "On the contrary…" and "I answer that".
- An index of all articles by topic, with title search; at the end of each article, "Keep reading" and an invitation to the track.
- Table of contents, reading progress bar and reading time computed at build time.
- Accent color that changes with the liturgical season (green, purple, white and gold, red).
- An "Our commitment" page, with a discreet link at the end of articles to suggest corrections.
- Dark theme by default, with a light mode saved in the browser, and an RSS feed generated at build time.

## Technical decisions

- **Restart instead of patching**: a content site's value is in being found and shared, and an SPA ships empty HTML. With static generation every page is ready for Google and for link previews, and the old site's URLs redirect to their new place.
- **Content with a schema**: articles, topics, tracks and artworks are Nuxt Content collections validated at build time. Publishing means adding a file, not touching a component.
- **Review against the sources**: `npm run verificar` checks structure, spelling (LanguageTool), links, whether each Bible reference exists in the Nova Vulgata on vatican.va, literal Catechism quotes, and whether each Catechism link points to the page of the cited paragraph. An article is only published with no errors.
- **A doctrinal review per article**: before publishing, each text gets an internal review modeled on an ecclesiastical censor's (the weight of each claim, the sources and the ambiguities), kept in the repository. It is an internal review and is not presented as an *imprimatur*.
- **Art with provenance**: artworks come only from Wikimedia Commons, and `npm run obra` reads license, artist and source straight from there. Each work stores its collection, license, alt text for screen readers and the crop used in each format. No stock photos and no AI-generated images.
- **Computed liturgical calendar**: the season comes from the Roman calendar with the transfers used in Brazil, covered by tests.

## What I learned

- Choosing the architecture by product type: content calls for static rendering, not an SPA.
- Treating content as code, with a schema, validation and automated review against the sources.
- Building a visual identity that teaches alongside the text: sacred art as part of the content, not decoration.
